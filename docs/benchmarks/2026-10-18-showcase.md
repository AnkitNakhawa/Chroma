# 1080p motion showcase — 2026-10-18

This is a complete product-workload measurement, rather than a tiny graphics
fixture: an 18-second editorial film with coastal footage, animated shaped text,
native cards, crossfades and an original electronic score. It encodes 540
1920×1080 frames at 30 fps, plus 864,000 sample frames of 48 kHz stereo audio,
into H.264/AAC MP4. See [reproduction and credits](../../examples/showcase/README.md).

## Environment and method

- Apple M5, 16 GiB RAM; macOS 26.6 (25G72); AppleClang 21; FFmpeg 9.0.2.
- CMake Release build, CPU renderer, FFmpeg and HarfBuzz/FreeType enabled.
- Workload revision `7390fff`; engine code unchanged from `92e0afe`.
- Asset download, 1080p/30 normalization, font acquisition and score synthesis
  are excluded. Source checksums are enforced; the generated manifest records
  all prepared-asset hashes. The raw report records the exact composition hash.
- One initial full encode served as a discarded warm-up; it preceded a minor
  caption-baseline adjustment. All six measured runs used the same final
  composition and prepared assets, with a warm filesystem cache.
- Three fresh-process full renders per variant, in order C/U, U/C, C/U. No
  concurrent render/test workload was launched during measurements. These are
  local, non-isolated machine measurements, not a statistical significance claim.
- Each sample uses `chroma benchmark --iterations 1`, which performs complete
  composition, mixing, encode and mux before deleting its temporary MP4. The
  harness captures wall time and `/usr/bin/time -l` maximum resident set size.
- Default budgets are 64 MiB image / 16 MiB text; the control sets both to zero.
  This workload uses video, native shapes and text; its image cache is unused.

## Results

| Variant | Median wall time | Throughput from median | Wall range | Maximum observed RSS |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| cached | 85.91 s | 6.29 fps | 85.42–89.51 s | 815.9 MiB |
| uncached | 89.37 s | 6.04 fps | 88.08–96.13 s | 786.5 MiB |

Caching improved median throughput by **4.0%** and reduced median
wall time by **3.9%**. The ranges overlap, and one paired comparison
favored the uncached path. This is a modest observed improvement; three samples
per variant do not establish a reliable general speed advantage.

The cached median is **4.77× the video's duration**: a polished film is
possible, but this CPU implementation is not real-time at 1080p. This result must
not be compared directly with historical 160×90 or 640×360 fixture throughput,
especially because those reports used a different host.

## Where the time goes

Mean stage times for the three cached runs:

| Stage | Mean time |
|---|---:|
| composition | 70.274 s |
| decode | 2.781 s |
| decode_conversion | 2.692 s |
| linear_to_rgb | 8.967 s |
| rgb_to_yuv | 0.911 s |
| codec_mux | 0.657 s |
| audio | 0.318 s |
| compile | 0.004 s |

These are leaf-stage timings. `execute`, `encode` and `total` in the raw profiles
are enclosing measurements and must not be added to these rows again.

All cached runs retain 30 text preparations, with 3,985 hits and 30 misses,
occupying 397,756 accounted bytes (0.38 MiB). The disabled runs prepare on all
4,015 requests. The process RSS is much larger than the prepared-text cache and
is not bounded by that cache budget; full-frame surfaces, decoder/encoder state,
allocator behavior and temporary work also contribute. RSS varies and does not
show a demonstrated memory improvement from enabling caching here.

Composition dominates, rather than the final codec/mux stage. Source inspection
shows text and shape rendering still materializes canvas-sized surfaces and
applies effects/composites over those surfaces. This motivates a bounded-region
text/shape execution experiment, evaluated against this exact film and the CPU
reference, before assuming codec acceleration will fix the bottleneck.

## Correctness and scope

The delivery verifier independently decodes every frame with FFmpeg, checks all
540 presentation timestamps, exact A/V durations and decoded audio sample count,
level/fade behavior, and four scene frames with caches enabled/disabled. Native
frames must match exactly; their H.264 counterparts must have mean absolute RGB
error below 3/255. [Recorded validation](2026-10-18-showcase-verification.json) and the
[asset manifest](2026-10-18-showcase-assets.json) accompany the showcase.

[Raw samples, per-stage timings, counters and memory](2026-10-18-showcase-results.json)
include every measured run. This A/B isolates prepared-resource retention; both
variants use current decode-conversion and eligible CPU fusion optimizations.
It does not measure their historical cumulative speedup. CUDA remains
unimplemented, and no equivalent FFmpeg or GPU-configured Remotion comparison
was conducted for this film.
